## What was built

- **`src/world/streaming-plan.ts`** — the pure rules: render distance clamp (2–32), rings (generate RD+1, mesh RD, keep meshes RD+1, keep data RD+2), Chebyshev distance, ring offsets, the smoothed travel direction (`TravelTracker`), the priority key (ring, in-frustum, travel dot product, distance), `neighboursReady`, `canFreeData`.
- **`src/world/streamer.ts`** — the one owner of column state (`queuedGen / generating / generated / queuedMesh / meshing / meshed`). Works against a host interface so it is unit-tested with a fake host. Contains the planner (at most once per frame, when the camera column or the view changed), stale-job cancellation (counted), serials and tokens so late results are ignored, the deferred-upload queue, region requests with pins, hysteresis, the pump (a timer task that does work between frames in slices of about 3 ms), the mesh attempts (`beginMeshAttempt` / `endMeshAttempt`: a re-mesh frees the section meshes its attempt did not produce), and bounded generation retries (`genFailed`).
- **`src/world/world-manager.ts`** — `createWorld` keeps its barrier (generate and light radius 5, mesh radius 4, no terrain drawn while loading) and then lets the streamer fill up to RD; `waitForTerrain(r)` is a region request (`Streamer.moveTo` first, because with `requestAnimationFrame` held no frame tells the streamer where the camera is); `render()` runs `streamer.update`, then uploads for 3 ms; `setRenderDistance`, `getStreamingStats`; the streamer host adapter (`uploadSection` records the section of the current attempt, `endMeshAttempt` frees the rest).
- **`src/render/frustum.ts`, `src/render/chunk-renderer.ts`** — column-level frustum culling (conservative column AABB against the six planes, margin 0.01 block), the same function for the draw and for the in-frustum priority; `removeColumnMeshesExcept` (keeps the sections of the current attempt, frees the others, releases their tint references).
- **`src/render/fenced-error-drain.ts`, `src/main.ts`** — WebGL error drain without a blocking round trip (see decisions).
- **`src/engine/frame-stats.ts`, `src/debug/api/streaming.ts`** — `setRenderDistance`, `getStreamingStats`, `resetFrameStats`, `getFrameStats` (`frameCpuMsP95/Max`, `uploadMsP95/Max`, `glCheckMsP95/Max`, `pumpMsP95/Max`), `getGlResourceCounts`, `getRenderStats` (`chunksLoaded` = columns with block data, `chunksMeshed` = columns completely meshed, `chunksVisible` = section meshes of the columns drawn, see Known limitations).
- **`tests/e2e/helpers/flight.ts`** — `fly()` (page-side loop, wall-clock speed, waits two frames at the end), `waitForStreamingIdle()`.

## Known limitations

- **`uploadMsP95` and `frameCpuMsP95` are above the M22a budgets on SwiftShader** (5.2–10.8 ms and 8.6–12.3 ms in the ten flights; 6.5 and 9.5 in the final local verify). See "Notes for M22a".
- **A 50 ms assertion on wall-clock time is exposed to CPU contention.** With 2 Playwright workers (before master set `workers: 1`) on this 4-core machine, a step that takes 0.3 ms alone (storing one section, copying one padded section) was measured at 25–131 ms when the OS preempted the renderer. Tasks were already cut to single sections for that reason (pump max fell from 277 ms to 53–58 ms under load), but a miss of a few ms remains possible there: in two plain verify runs with 2 workers the flight test failed on `pumpMsMax` 53.3 and 57.8 (and once earlier on 161). With one worker it was 11.9–20.5 ms in the ten standalone flights, 16.5 in the `CI=1` run, 23.4 in the local verify on eaaf8e9, 20.1 in the final local verify and 9.7 in CI on eaaf8e9.
- **`glCheckMsMax`** (blocking `getError` when four frames are in flight) ranged 1.4–2.1 s across the runs above: 1402.8 in the local verify on eaaf8e9, 1806.1 in CI on eaaf8e9, 1925.1 in the final local verify, 1847–2057 in the ten standalone flights. It is the time the GPU needs for the frames before it (SwiftShader), counted outside `frameCpuMs` as the owner decided.
- **Streaming work outside the timed slices.** `pumpMs` covers the pump's slices and the worker-result tasks. Not included: a generation rejection (`genFailed`), a failed mesh starter (`prepStep`'s error path), and the synchronous calls `setRenderDistance`, `requestRegion` (plan and dispatch) and `abandonGenerations` (called by `setWorkerPoolSize`). They run in the caller's task and were not measured. `setRenderDistance` and `requestRegion` plan the whole ring, so they take longer at a larger render distance.
- **Tint re-upload runs in the frame callback.** `ChunkRenderer.render()` calls `ColumnTintCache.getColumnTints`, which re-uploads a column's grass and foliage textures (two `texImage2D` calls, 16×16 RGB each) when the column's tint arrays were replaced after its textures were created. The cost is in `frameCpuMs`, not in `uploadMs` and not under the 3 ms upload budget. The flight does not isolate it, so its share of `frameCpuMs` is unknown.
- **What `chunksVisible` counts.** `ChunkRenderer.render()` sets it at the end of each call to the number of uploaded section meshes of the columns whose box touches the frustum (column-level culling, 0.01-block margin). It counts section meshes, not buckets or draw calls: a section with no geometry is counted, the three passes and the wireframe overlay do not change it, and a section that is not uploaded yet is not counted.
- **Mesh failures are silent.** A failed mesh job leaves its column unmeshed; it is neither retried nor reported. Generation failures are retried up to three tries and then reported with `console.error`. Mesh failures need the same treatment; not done here.
- A column's mesh jobs are started section by section, so on a loaded machine the first meshes appear a little later than when a whole column was started at once.

## Notes for dependent tasks

- **M04b** (fade-in, memory): column state is `Streamer.phaseOf(cx, cz)`; a column reaches `meshed` after its last section was uploaded (`StreamerHost.uploadSection` is the single upload place; `freeMesh` the single GPU-free place). Fade-in can start when `uploadSection` is called for a column's first section. A re-mesh that produces fewer sections frees the missing ones when it completes (`endMeshAttempt`); a column's old meshes stay visible until then.
- **M05a/M05b** (lighting): the streamed columns get no light computation. The only light job is the one `createWorld`'s barrier enqueues (`enqueueLightRegionJob` around the spawn). Columns the streamer generates later have no computed light, so their mesh jobs read the storage defaults. M05b must light a streamed column before its mesh job runs. Light data is freed with its column (`World.removeColumn` → `LightEngine.removeColumn`), so a column streamed back in has no light until M05b computes it again.
- **M06a** (player): `fly()` in `tests/e2e/helpers/flight.ts` is the one place that sets the camera; switch it to teleport the player. The streamer reads the camera once per frame in `WorldManager.render()` through `streamerView(camera)`; after placing the camera without frames (a test holding `requestAnimationFrame`) call `waitForTerrain`, which calls `Streamer.moveTo`.
- **M05+** (edits): call `Streamer.invalidate(cx, cz)` after changing blocks so the column is meshed again; mesh jobs for edits should be enqueued with `background = false` so they use the reserved worker.
- Invariant (unit-tested after every step): a column with a mesh, or whose mesh jobs are running, has all eight neighbour columns loaded. Data is freed only when no neighbour holds a mesh (`canFreeData`).
- `getStreamingStats()` fields: `renderDistance, genQueued, genRunning, meshQueued, meshRunning, uploadsDeferred, cancelledJobs`.

## Notes for M22a (what limits p95 in this environment)

- **One section upload costs 6–10 ms on SwiftShader** (`bufferData` of the section's vertex and index data, plus the VAO setup). The frame always uploads at least one section, so `uploadMsP95` is the cost of one section upload, above the 3 ms budget. To get under 3 ms the upload unit must be smaller than a section (one bucket per call, or sub-allocation from a big buffer with `bufferSubData`).
- **Draw submission of about 740 visible sections costs about 5 ms** (3 GL calls per section). Section-level culling and batching (merged buffers, multi-draw) are what lower it; column-level culling already keeps it to the columns in view.
- **The GPU runs at about 1.3 frames per second at RD 8** (0.4–0.8 s per frame). `gl.getError` and `bufferData` block the main thread while the GPU is behind (the reason for the fenced drain and for the rule that uploads happen only inside the frame budget).
- **Fence status becomes visible about 20 frames late** in Chrome (sampled at each frame start, 18–24 fences reported unsignalled while a blocking `getError` showed a backlog of about two frames), so uploads cannot be gated on "the GPU has caught up": with that gate 269 uploads stayed deferred for 120 s. Not done; see the decisions file.
- Cost to expect from M22a work on this machine: the budget of 8 ms `frameCpuMsP95` at RD 8 needs the draw submission and the upload unit to shrink together.

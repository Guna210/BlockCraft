  **Why this frame does not fully meet its checklist** ("at most a few chunks still loading at the far edge, near terrain fully present"): the gaps are in the middle distance, not only at the far edge, and they are not a few chunks. The cause is the streaming speed on SwiftShader, from the M04a notes: one section upload costs 6–10 ms (`uploadMsP95` was 5.2–10.8 ms across ten flights), so a frame can upload about one section; the GPU runs at about 1.3 frames per second at RD 8 (0.4–0.8 s per frame); and drawing ~740 sections costs about 5 ms. Streaming cannot keep up with a 30 blocks/s flight here, so the frame shows the streaming state. The orchestrator decided to keep it as a known limitation for this PR. **M22a is asked to retake this frame** once draw submission and upload cost are lower.

## Known limitations

- **Heap test blocked.** `performance.memory` did not move with a 64 MB allocation under the current config (`playwright.config.ts` launches without `--enable-precise-memory-info`). Two readings were identical to the byte (19,300,000 B), so a growth check against that reading passes for any heap. My earlier note in this file said Chrome reports 100 KB steps; the probe result is what the test relies on, and it contradicts the 100 KB account. The orchestrator's account (buckets of about 6 %, cached for up to 20 minutes) fits the reading, but I have not verified the cache duration. The fix is a launch-flag decision for master, which the owner is taking. The probe stays as the check that the reading responds.
- **Fast-flight frame does not meet its checklist** (see Screenshots and the reasons above). Kept as a known limitation for this PR; M22a to retake.
- **Hole finder seeds from the top row only** (as specified). A frame whose top row has no sky reports all sky as enclosed (the fast-flight frame). If the checks need to cover such frames, the seed set has to change, which is a decision for the orchestrator.
- **Horizon hole check** covers the whole frame, so it also looks at the middle of the frame, where the far chunks are. It does not prove that the far edge at the corners is correct, only that no enclosed hole larger than 64 px exists.
- **The fade is rarely seen under SwiftShader.** A frame takes about 0.75 s here, so a 400 ms fade usually ends before the next frame. The dither is visible in the fast-flight frame at the edges of the gaps (the speckled band), which is the one place this run shows it. No e2e test checks a pixel mid-fade; the dither itself is covered by unit tests of the table.
- **A column whose generation failed three times still blocks its neighbours** (the known M04a limitation). No test here hit it.
- **Mesh failures are still silent** (M04a limitation).
- **A column whose meshes are all freed by a stale completion fades again** when it comes back: `finishIfDone`'s stale branch frees the column's meshes, so the column disappears and fades back in. That is outside decision 2 as written and is not changed here.

## Notes for dependent tasks

- **M22a** (culling and batching): retake `m04-fast-flight.png` once streaming keeps up at RD 8 (the frame in this PR is a known limitation). `ChunkRenderer.render()` sets `u_fade` per column in each pass; a batched draw must keep the fade per column (one uniform per column, or per batch of columns with equal fade).
- **M05b** (lighting): a column's fade starts at its first section upload (`uploadSection` in `world-manager.ts`). If M05b changes a column's light after that upload, the change shows during the fade, as it does for any mesh update. Nothing in M04b depends on the light data.
- **Debug API:** `getStreamingStats()` returns `WorldStreamingStats`; `columnsFading` is time-based, read at the call. A test that waits for it to reach zero should still wait two frames before a screenshot.
- **Attempts:** `StreamerHost.abandonMeshAttempt` must be implemented by any new host; the streamer calls it from `discardAttempt` only while a column is `meshing`.
- **Heap test:** `window.gc` needs `--js-flags=--expose-gc`, already in `playwright.config.ts`. The test throws if it is missing. The probe needs a reading that responds to allocation, which depends on the launch-flag decision.

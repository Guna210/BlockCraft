# M05b — Smooth lighting and AO decisions

All decisions below were supplied by the owner. The later approval permits decoder-only changes in the two legacy unit files, with no assertion changes.

1. Streamed light is computed from a ready rectangle plus a full column border, committing only the interior. A one-block skirt supplies exact light when a neighbour is unlit. Light completion never remeshes neighbours. Stages are generated → lit → meshed; the light and mesh rings coincide. Generation/free rings remain unchanged. Per-cell work runs in workers, above-height sections share a sky sentinel, opaque non-emitting sections are zero, and light is freed with columns. Edits retain incremental BFS restricted to lit columns. The createWorld region barrier remains; a unit test compares it cell for cell with batched lighting.
2. RGB propagates/removes independently and combines by channel max. The scalar block-light cache remains equal to the RGB maximum. Tint uses rounded inverse-brightness offsets, tested per emitter. Torch emits 14 with pale warm RGB proportions (1, 0.8, 0.55); lumite ore emits its existing 12 in cool cyan; lava emits 15 in orange-red. No lamp or glowcap placeholders.
3. Revision of `M02b-vertex-format.md`: word 0 remains x/y/z (5 bits each), normal (3), AO (2), sky (4), block R (4), tint (4). Word 1 is tile index bits 0–11, u bits 12–16, v bits 17–21, block G bits 22–25, block B bits 26–29, spare bits 30–31. Source, shader and test decoding share constants. An atlas-size test fails at 4096 tiles.
4. Each corner averages the non-opaque outward face/side/side/corner samples, dropping the corner when both side samples are opaque. AO is 3 when both sides are opaque, otherwise the sum of side and corner occlusion. Diagonals compare corner light/AO scores and are symmetry-tested. Greedy merging requires identical sky/R/G/B/AO tuples at all four corners and across merged faces. Plants/models use own-cell light without AO; water uses smooth light without AO. Below-world padded light is zero.
5. Superseded during the owner-requested shader-cost optimization: the original implementation interpolated light before the fragment brightness curve `b(L) = 0.04 + 0.96 L/15`. Named constants also define AO, with AO zero equal to one. Texture colour multiplies the per-channel maximum of sky brightness times the day multiplier and RGB brightness, then AO and existing face shading. The day multiplier defaults to one and has a renderer setter for M12.
6. Standing torch uses an original procedural cross sprite and `data/blocks/light-sources.json`. Integer corner positions are retained. No item, icon or wall torch is introduced.
7. A separate debug API module provides `setDebugLight(on)`, off by default. It overrides brightness to one while retaining AO and face shading. The owner-named test calls surround the two M03 cave screenshots and, by the later explicit approval, only the M04b fast-flight screenshot. The fixed-height camera at x500 is inside a cave; fullbright makes the M04 coverage review independent of correct underground darkness. Other tasks' committed screenshots are restored after verification.
8. M05 visual tests use a sealed stone room and an open-roof stone inner corner. They retain the supplied dark/rise/hue/AO thresholds, record camera and room geometry, and save the two named screenshots under M05b. Unit tests cover decisions 1–4.
9. Stage counters expose completed generation, light, mesh and upload columns, queue depths and cancellation counts. Flight throughput compares master and branch at the same speed. Every stage prioritises distance; light jobs are bounded independently and preserve generation/mesh worker capacity. A stale column is rejected individually within a batch. Idle detection includes light work; adopted region light can still queue a subsequent mesh request. All M04 tests retain their assertions and thresholds.

Edits additionally refresh affected columns' worker-computed skirts. The incremental BFS remains immediate; the refresh prevents stale skirts beside unlit neighbours and rejects an in-flight snapshot invalidated by a later edit. An unlit border edit also invalidates nearby snapshots that read that border, even though the guarded incremental BFS has no touched sections there. A deduplicated column set preserves per-column rejection rather than cancelling the batch.

The initial region computes from the generated outer ring but commits only its interior, so later outer-ring meshes obtain an exact skirt from their ready border. Ready streaming strips may run along X or Z. Waiting uploads are re-prioritised along with mesh work, and draining upload backpressure resumes mesh dispatch immediately.

For rendering cost, the worker labels meshes whose RGB components are all zero. Those use an algebraically identical sky-only fragment formula, including the minimum brightness of the zero block-light channels. Other meshes use the full RGB formula. Face shade is constant per quad and computed once in the vertex shader; sky/RGB/AO still interpolate. Sky/AO share a two-component varying on zero-RGB sections; material IDs share one flat integer. Tint coordinates include the constant face offset before interpolation, and untinted faces perform no biome texture lookup. Sampling reads the shared nine-cell face stencil once, with byte-separated channels for safe four-sample sums.

The debug-light setter presents a frame synchronously, so fullbright also takes effect while a caller holds the normal animation loop. Debug wireframe outline buffers are created on first use, rather than alongside every terrain upload; their contents and mesh lifetime remain unchanged.

## Current streaming and rendering invariants

The owner corrected the earlier master-parity guardrails: draw cadence, visible sections, column throughput and cancels are comparison evidence, not SPEC pass/fail criteria. Completion still requires unchanged full verification, runtime guards, visual review and green PR CI. A sole M04b not-blank failure requires full same-container master verification. No draw gating, skipped/deferred terrain draws or merge relaxation is permitted. Existing tests retain their assertions, thresholds and timing. The sole newly approved M04 test change is setDebugLight(true) immediately before fast-flight screenshot capture and setDebugLight(false) immediately after, with no other change in that test.

The terrain draw gate was rejected under AGENTS §6 and removed together with its tests. It skipped terrain draws while counting update-only callbacks as rendered frames. Its passing verify is invalid closure evidence. The current callback is synchronous: native dedicated-worker message handlers deliver results between frames, and streaming/upload processing precedes the draw; no MessageChannel turn or promise suspension precedes each draw. Count and time only actual rendered frames. Existing initial-world loading behavior remains; it is not expanded to stabilize tests.

The owner withdrew the shared physical budget because SPEC §3.2 and M02c require max(2, hardwareConcurrency − 1) real mesh workers. WorldManager now constructs separate mesh, generation and light pools with their unchanged master defaults: each has max(2, hardwareConcurrency − 1) real workers. There is no SharedWorkerBudget in the game path, no virtual count and no fixed gen2/light1 cap. Initial/foreground mesh work actually uses every mesh worker; background work preserves master's edit reserve. The existing shared-budget helper tests cover the historical opt-in utility, not the production pool sizing.

Nearest-chain ranks are forwarded to the actual dedicated pool queues; foreground mesh jobs retain priority. All light workers receive ready disjoint batches in parallel. Compare committed interior rectangles against every posted job before dispatch; overlapping borders/skirt reads are allowed, overlapping interiors stay queued. A blocked near batch does not stop unrelated disjoint ready work. Native completions retain job-ID correlation; Streamer serial/phase checks reject stale results and own each column's commit.

The owner explicitly authorized changing `tests/unit/m05-light-scheduling.test.ts`: its former single-active-worker assertions encoded the withdrawn shared-budget instruction. The replacement verifies all N workers receive work, checks every in-flight pair has disjoint interiors, checks queued conflicting jobs and exact-once replies, and compares both completion orders against `computeRegionLight` plus full batch RGB/skirt results. No M02 assertion, expected value or test case is changed.

While moving, select the nearest 16 unfinished columns, frustum first and existing travel/distance priority next. Pull each target's generated 3×3 light border before far generation, then light and mesh the target. A ready contiguous strip may join a light batch (at most 8 columns), but one ready target starts immediately without waiting for a large rectangle. Refresh view before refreshing chains. Stop restricting dispatch to the moving focus set when motion stops, so stationary heap/horizon waits drain all outstanding work.

Keep the existing 3 ms upload budget. Sort uploads by focus tier, column rank/distance, then section height; complete a near column rather than scattering uploads across farther columns. Upload backpressure releases mesh dispatch. Cancellation remains outside wanted rings; edit-serial invalidation rejects genuinely stale inputs independently of distance.

Generation transfers packed palette, ref-count and index buffers; ChunkSection adopts them without rebuilding voxel storage on the main thread. Light input transfers compact live-column snapshots, copies one typed section atomically in 1 ms slices, and expands the rectangle in the worker. Workers build padded blocks/light from transferred neighbouring sections. Obsolete dense light-column storage was removed. The shared 3 ms streaming deadline is checked between generation/light/mesh stage groups; freeing removes one section per step. Atomic native operations can overshoot soft deadlines; record real wall time without filtering it.

Terrain retains indexed quads with shared triangle indices, two uint attributes and 8 bytes per vertex. AO diagonal normalization happens in workers. Per-section culling remains conservative; empty/light-only records are not drawn. Brightness, tint, AO and sky-uniform evaluation happens at vertices; fragment work samples atlas/cutout/fade/grass-side coverage and multiplies interpolated values. No per-fragment light unpacking, loops or pow.

No diagnostic GPU timer queries, query polling, readPixels, clientWaitSync or finish remain in shipped source. The query-free GL lifecycle audit found fewer total branch buffer/texture allocations and deletes than master, and no additional framebuffer/renderbuffer or canvas-resize lifecycle. The existing fenced error drain and tint cache remain master's lifecycle. Chromium shared-image teardown wall durations vary; a causal source defect was not established.
